# Accountability

Accountability is taking business risk under your own name. In Naval Ravikant's wealth model, society rewards visible responsibility with responsibility, equity, and leverage.

## Key Ideas

- Accountability is not only doing work; it is accepting name-attached consequences for judgment.
- The most accountable people often have singular, public, risky brands.
- Accountability makes [[wiki/concepts/Specific Knowledge]] legible: others can see who made the judgment and who should receive trust, blame, upside, or downside.
- Without accountability, leverage can magnify someone else's decisions while leaving you as rented time. ^[inferred]

## Risk And Reward

Accountability is uncomfortable because it exposes judgment to public evaluation. That exposure is also the routing mechanism that lets reputation, equity, and opportunity compound. ^[inferred]

This connects to [[wiki/topics/Technical Management]] and [[wiki/topics/Critical Thinking]]: judgment improves when decisions have consequences and feedback, not when the decision-maker can remain invisible. ^[inferred]

## Software Ownership Triad

[[wiki/sources/Coding Is Not Solved Source Guide|Coding Is Not Solved]] frames accountable software ownership as three coupled conditions:

1. **Knowledge** — the owner understands the problem, system behavior, important limits, and likely failure modes.
2. **Mandate** — the owner has enough authority to make, reject, revert, and repair consequential decisions.
3. **Accountability** — the owner remains answerable when the system causes harm or fails to meet its commitments.

Removing any one condition breaks ownership. Accountability without knowledge assigns blame without control; knowledge without mandate creates responsibility without authority; mandate without accountability allows decisions without consequences. ^[inferred]

AI changes authorship but does not terminate the accountability chain. A model can generate, inspect, or operate software, yet the human or organization deploying it remains responsible for risk acceptance, production operation, and recovery. The article distinguishes a tool being *responsible* for an action from a human being *accountable* for consequences, but legal and organizational vocabularies do not use these terms consistently. ^[ambiguous]

This is why [[wiki/concepts/Cognitive Debt]] is also an accountability problem: when no human can reconstruct intent or reason about failure, the organization still bears consequences but has lost the knowledge needed to exercise ownership. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- What forms of accountability are healthy, and what forms turn into status performance?
- How should accountability work in teams where outcomes are genuinely collective?
- How should knowledge, mandate, and consequences be distributed when humans approve work that agents generated and verified?
